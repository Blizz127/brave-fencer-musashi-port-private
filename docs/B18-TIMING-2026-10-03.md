# B18 overlay timing at `main_0012:8014C99C` (2026-10-03)

**Result:** the B18 attract DEVOBS difference came from the deny reference
leg, not from native code. The native leg delivers the IRQ at its due cycle.
The deny leg holds it for one delay slot because of a call boundary that
denying the function creates. Under the refined proof rule below, B18 passes
village and attract. It may enter the next private candidate (r41+). It was
in no build before this.

## The 48 lines

They form two hunks of 24 records. In each hunk:

- the native leg records the IRQ delivery (`D`) at pc `8014c9fc`. That is the
  `nop` in the delay slot of `8014C99C`'s `jr ra`;
- the deny leg records the same delivery at `801462b0`, the resume pc after
  the `jal 8014C99C` at `801462a8` in its only caller, `80146128`;
- every record in the hunk is the same apart from that pc, and the deny leg
  is exactly +2 cycles later (the slot `nop`'s cost). The streams are equal
  again after the hunk.

## Mechanism

`80146128` is native in every leg. With `8014C99C` denied, the native caller
reaches the interpreted callee through a thunk (`musashi_native_guest_call`).
The callee then returns to the lane's sentinel: in its `jr ra` delay slot,
npc is `0xFFFFFFFF`. No exception can resume there, so
`pc_port/irq_scheduler.c` holds the IRQ (the "Hold the IRQ" block, pinned by
`irq_scheduler_probe` case 9). It is delivered once the call has returned, at
the caller's resume pc (`musashi_native_guest_call`'s resume checkpoint),
one slot later.

With `8014C99C` native, the call is a direct native-to-native call and there
is no sentinel. The deadline guard (D-011) delivers the IRQ in the slot at its
due cycle. A fully interpreted run would do the same, because there npc is
the real return address. **The native leg is the faithful one.** The handover
called this "3 IRQs delivered 2 cycles early". The symptom was right, but the
reading was reversed: the deny leg is 2 cycles late.

The control proves it. When `80146128` is also interpreted in both legs, the
call edge is interpreted on both sides and the streams are byte-identical.

## Refined route-proof rule

A batch passes when, on the village and attract routes with the eager epoch,
native vs deny has identical RAM and PAD, and DEVOBS is either identical or
differs **only** by thunk-return IRQ holds. `tools/devobs_route_compare.py`
decides this mechanically from the retail words. Every hunk must have the
same records and fields, a `D` pair at (native: a `jr ra` slot, deny: a
`jal`'s resume pc), and one constant shift equal to the slot instruction's
interpreter cost. Anything else is DIFFER. When a hold is found, the
preferred evidence is still a control leg that also denies the native
callers, as here. `tests/test_devobs_route_compare.py` pins the rule.

This rule does not waive native-vs-deny. It names the one known oracle
artifact, and it rejects every other difference.

## Probe regression

`tests/native_lane_overlay_probe.c` now runs the D-011 deadline guard on
overlay entries: 32 schedules per positive pattern, comparing the delivery
pc, slot, instruction, cycle and total. Result: `main_0012` 58 functions the
same, 0 different, 810 cases; `main_0003` 3 the same, 0 different.
`8014C99C` with its full path planted is the same on every schedule. The
synthetic probe has no thunk sentinel, so it could not reproduce the route
difference. That is consistent with the native body being exact.

## Limits

- Still hybrid. This changes nothing about the native-completion status.
  Overlay and scene code are mostly interpreted, 48 functions keep the fixed
  cycle charge, and the KUSEG gap is open.
- `8012B608` (ambiguous cycle map) and `8012DEB8` (KUSEG null pointer) stay
  in `OVERLAY_DENY`. `80128ED8` stays refused (layout).
- The thunk-return hold is a property of every native caller of a denied or
  unadmitted interpreted callee. It also exists in the r37 release wherever
  native code thunk-calls interpreted code. It delays an IRQ by one slot
  relative to retail. Disclosed as D-043.
